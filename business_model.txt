The business model I'd consider
I'd make the initial engagement very concrete.
Step 1 — Workflow audit
Spend 1–2 weeks inside the customer's business.
Understand:

What software they use
Where employees spend time
What gets copied between systems
What requires repetitive decisions
What information is trapped in email/documents
Where mistakes happen
What the owner personally does every day
Step 2 — Find the highest-value bottleneck
Don't try to "AI-ify everything."
Find something like:

"Your sales team spends 30 hours/week manually qualifying leads."
Then calculate:
30 hours × $40/hour × 52 = $62,400/year

If you can eliminate 70% of that work, suddenly your project has an obvious ROI.

Step 3 — Build it
This is where your FDE team shines.
You integrate:

LLMs
APIs
databases
CRM
email
spreadsheets
internal tools
web apps
automation
agents
into a workflow that actually works.
Step 4 — Stay embedded
This is the part I think could become your moat.
Don't disappear after delivering software.

Keep watching:

"What's still annoying?"
"What broke?"
"What are people doing manually?"
"What new process should we automate?"
You're essentially becoming the company's external AI engineering team.
And I think there's a really interesting long-term vision
Initially:
5 FDEs → 20 customers

But over time you learn that the same problems appear repeatedly.

For example:

Eventually you could turn your repeated solutions into your own software platform.
That's where the business gets much more interesting.

You start as an FDE studio.

Then:

Services → repeatable solutions → proprietary platform → SaaS

That's a potentially powerful trajectory.
